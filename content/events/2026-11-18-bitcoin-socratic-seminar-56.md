## Topics

### Bitcoin

#### rbitcoin

- [rbitcoin](https://github.com/rbitcoin-org/rbitcoin) - A Bitcoin full node in Rust for server-side use, AI-written (Grok) under Brandon Black's prompting. It keeps no UTXO set: blocks live in a relational archive, consensus and script are pure Rust with no `libbitcoinconsensus`, and the tx index, address index, Electrum and Esplora REST run in the same process, so there is no separate electrs or Fulcrum. Its consensus rules have been checked against Hornet's written rules and Rob Hamilton's btc-lean description. It ships as static musl binaries and a container image. Would you run an AI-written consensus node, and what review would it take to trust one?

#### rbitcoin.space

- [rbitcoin.space](https://rbitcoin.space/) - A block explorer that runs beside a stock rbitcoin node: one Rust server, a Solid frontend, and an Esplora-compatible API for mainnet, signet, testnet4 and Testnet 5. Because the node already keeps the tx index, address index, cluster mempool and per-transaction fees, there is no second indexer, no second mempool and no database server, just SQLite for the little the node does not store. It shows the live mempool and projected blocks, mining pools, the Lightning graph through a gossip-only LDK peer that holds no funds, Ark VTXOs, and silent payments addresses, and has a read-only MCP server for agents. It also speaks mempool.space's WebSocket protocol, so mempool.space's own frontend can run on top. Also on Tor. [Code](https://github.com/smolcars/rbitcoin-space). How much of an explorer stack can the node itself absorb?

#### Testnet 5

- [Testnet 5 is live](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/DmFiHeo-xRw) - The replacement for testnet4 launched on November 14, as specified in [BIP 95](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0095.md) by Pol Espinasa and Fabian Jahr, now marked Deployed. It drops the 20-minute minimum-difficulty exception whose abuse caused testnet4's block storms, raises the minimum difficulty about six orders of magnitude so you need an ASIC to mine, and enforces BIP 54 Consensus Cleanup from block 1, so miners can check their software follows the new rules. For a fair launch, Anthony Towns [ran a public ckpool](https://gist.github.com/ajtowns/ebb148819c6028387271e6f0dce5e1da) to mine the genesis block, whose message commits to mainnet block 975744 to rule out a pre-mine. Network magic is `FIVE`, and the P2P port is 18353. You can watch it on [rbitcoin.space](https://rbitcoin.space/testnet5/). Will testnet5's coins stay worthless, now that every miner needs an ASIC?

#### Bitcoin Core

- [Bitcoin Core fast-tracks easy-to-find bugs](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/70VSp_ARD5Q) - On October 22, Antoine Poinsot announced a change to Bitcoin Core's security policy. Reports of bugs that are easy to find or rediscover are rising, because LLMs make it cheap to hunt some bug classes and to spot fixes that were merged quietly. When triage flags a bug as easy to discover, such as one a simple LLM prompt could surface, Core no longer holds the fix for the next major release behind a long embargo: it backports the fix to every maintained release, ships as soon as possible, and publishes an advisory with it. The first three advisories went out on October 28 ([Optech](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/30/)): a peer could exhaust a non-Linux node's memory by sending TCP urgent data, announcements queued for paused pre-BIP31 peers could grow without bound, and a miner could, impractically, corrupt other nodes' undo data so that a precisely timed reorg crashes them. Long embargoes gave slow upgraders time: do they still help, when an attacker's LLM can find the same bug?
- [Bitcoin Core 32.1](https://bitcoincore.org/en/releases/32.1/) - Those fixes landed after 32.0 was tagged, so 32.0 was never released: 32.1 is the release #55's rc3 became, out at the end of October alongside 31.2, 30.4 and 29.5. Beyond the changes in that rc (the mempool-based fee estimator, 200 default connections, global transaction relay rate limits, private broadcast fixes), the PSBT-creating RPCs now default to version 2 PSBTs, `exportwatchonlywallet` makes a watch-only copy of a descriptor wallet to restore on another node, and a rebuilt `-txindex` takes less than half the disk space. Anthropic Security is among the credited contributors. 32.1 also ended 29.x's life, so on November 10 Poinsot [disclosed three older bugs](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/tOLTRSOz87k) fixed in 29.2 and 30.0: TRUC sibling eviction during a reorg could stop a mining node from building block templates until restarted ([CVE-2025-62861](https://bitcoincore.org/en/2026/11/10/disclose-cve-2025-62861/), high severity), inbound Tor peers could inherit `-whitelist` permissions, and the first peer to announce a compact block could stall block processing for up to 10 minutes by malleating its witness. All affected versions are now end of life.

#### UTXO Set Proofs

- [A STARK proof of Bitcoin's UTXO set](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/a-stark-proof-of-bitcoins-utxo-set-at-block-972-500-with-circuits-proven-in-lean/3070) - l-henri posted a 1.6 MB "seal" covering three things: the header chain from genesis, a replay of blocks 935,001 to 972,500, and the UTXO set that results. It uses Stwo circle STARKs, with circuits written in Lean 4. It verifies in about half a second in a browser, and checking a 9.39 GB `dumptxoutset` snapshot against it took 72 seconds, nearly all of it spent reading the file. Lean proofs cover the header rules, SHA-256 against its FIPS spec, the block checks, and how the pieces compose, and the Lean specs were fuzzed against Core's C++ with zero mismatches (tested, not proven). The gaps are big: scripts and signatures are not checked, BIP68 relative locktimes and full sigop cost are still missing, so for now it guarantees less than assumevalid, and the replay starts from Core's assumeUTXO snapshot at block 935,000. Proving from genesis would take an estimated $1,000 to $1,500 of H100 time. A planned Core patch would accept a seal-attested snapshot and skip background validation. [Demo](https://starknet-innovation.github.io/starked-bitcoin), [code](https://github.com/starknet-innovation/starked-bitcoin). What would a proof have to cover before you would skip background validation on its word?

#### Soft Forks

- [BIPs 458, 459 and 460: cross-input signature aggregation](https://x.com/murchandamus/status/2118354588034347117) - Three drafts published together, announced by BIP editor Murch on November 5. [BIP 458](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0458.mediawiki) (Tim Ruffing, Jonas Nick, Fabian Jahr) specifies half-aggregation, which anyone can do without the signers' help and which shrinks n signatures from 64n bytes to 32n + 32. [BIP 459](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0459.mediawiki) (Jahr) specifies DahLIAS full aggregation, a two-round interactive protocol that produces one 64-byte signature however many signers take part. [BIP 460](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0460.mediawiki) (Jahr) is the soft fork that would use both, through a new witness v2 output type limited to Taproot-style key path spends. Inputs can opt out, which protects adaptor signature protocols that need a specific signature to appear on chain. BIP 459 estimates that full aggregation would have cut the average historical transaction's size by 20.5% and its weight by 7.3%, and a 150-input consolidation's weight by 27.4%. Activation parameters are undecided, the witness version may change, and the [Bitcoin Core implementation](https://github.com/fjahr/bitcoin/tree/bip460) is a work in progress. Coins would have to move to v2 outputs to benefit: are cheaper CoinJoins and consolidations enough to justify a soft fork?
- [Softfork before GTA VI?](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/softfork-before-gta-vi/2913) - Grand Theft Auto VI [comes out tomorrow](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/grand-theft-auto-vi-is-delayed-to-november-19-2026-211848530.html), November 19, after years of delays, and cmp_ancp's Delving thread asks whether Bitcoin can get a soft fork out first. Many protocols assume covenants are coming, yet attention drifted to inscriptions, Knots versus Core and post-quantum signatures, and the community never united behind CTV or TEMPLATEHASH. ZmnSCPxj suggested aiming for GTA VII instead. The likeliest candidate is BIP 54 Consensus Cleanup, already enforced on Testnet 5, and Antoine Riard [floated activation in the second half of 2027](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/TBBakeBK8_E), in a personal capacity, after more review, testing and wallet documentation. Adam Gibson's essay [Responses to Stagnation](https://insider.btcpp.dev/p/responses-to-stagnation-the-reactionary) argues the revolutionary energy now lives in cryptography that needs no new opcodes, like BitVM, Glock and his own [Lichen](https://github.com/AdamISZ/lichen), a single-issuer layer 2 whose users can redeem even if the issuer refuses or goes offline. Is stagnation a failure of process, or is building without soft forks the answer?

#### Quantum

- [Quantum landmines and vulnerable coin auctions](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/ARMwWdWcz9A) - Cory Fields's November 4 thought experiments try to make quantum theft a losing bet instead of freezing old coins. With a landmine, an owner commits ahead of time, in a small quantum-safe output, to a hidden proof of control over an old key. A thief who spends the old coins must lock up an equal amount of their own quantum-safe bitcoin, and during a challenge period (30 days in Fields's example) the owner can reveal the proof and take both. For coins whose keys are lost, a staked auction lets bidders register in advance against specific old outputs and win part of a thief's collateral, though the old coins themselves are destroyed, and a sats auction runs a bidding contest whenever a vulnerable coin moves, with no stake needed. All of it needs a quantum-safe output type and new consensus rules first ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382761/bitcoin-developer-quantum-landmines-losing-bet)). Other ideas this month: James Tagg's [QSAVE](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/5G38b3JL9fA) drafts would send unmigrated coins that cannot show a second factor, such as a zero-knowledge proof linking the seed to a post-quantum key, to recovery outputs held by custodians, and a [proof-of-decay](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/a-novel-post-quantum-approach-proof-of-decay-cryptographic-half-life-for-unmigrated-dormant-utxos/2970) proposal would shrink exposed coins on a schedule. Which is fairest to a holder who never moves: a freeze, a decay, or a bounty on the thief?
- [How much BTC already sits behind public keys](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/how-much-btc-sits-behind-keys-or-scripts-already-on-chain-block-975-019/3088) - A census duncan0k posted today: at block 975,019, 7,164,754 BTC, 35.6% of the 20,109,207 BTC in unspent outputs, sits behind a public key or script already visible on chain. P2PK outputs (1.71 million BTC) and Taproot outputs (222,545 BTC) count by their form alone, since the key is in the output. The other 73% of the exposed total is in hashed outputs (P2PKH, P2WPKH, P2SH and P2WSH) whose key or script was already revealed elsewhere on chain, usually through address reuse. A random sample of the outputs it did not count found none it had missed, and a full rescan reproduced its 33 GB of tables byte for byte. [Code](https://github.com/duncan0k/btc-exposure-index). If a third of the supply is already exposed, how much does any migration plan depend on getting address reusers to move?

#### Mining

- [Stratum V2 reaches the big pools](https://x.com/TheBlueMatt/status/2122321920192548963) - A big week for Sv2, as Matt Corallo laid out: Foundry announced it is [testing Sv2](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/56991), MARA Pool [mined its first Sv2 block](https://x.com/MARAFoundation_/status/2120520367500308580) on November 11, and Luxor announced Sv2 support. All three are pool-work mode: the pool still picks the transactions, and miners do not yet send their own templates through Job Declaration, as [DMND and GoMining did in June](https://www.tftc.io/stratum-v2-job-declaration-first-block-dmnd-gomining). Even so, pool-work Sv2 is the step before Job Declaration, it shows the industry is finally moving, and its encrypted, authenticated connections are a big security upgrade for all of Bitcoin. What will get the big pools to accept miner-built templates?
- [P2Poolv2 beta on Testnet 5](https://rbitcoin.space/testnet5/mining/pool/p2poolv2) - The [P2Poolv2](https://github.com/p2poolv2/p2poolv2) beta is working well on Testnet 5 and has mined about 29% of the blocks rbitcoin.space has seen there so far, second only to unidentified miners. Of this week's mining news it is the only immediate decentralization win: no pool operator picks the transactions, and miners are paid straight from the coinbase. Share trading for small miners is still being written, and there is no mainnet date yet.
- [Rune mints broke compact block relay](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/stats-on-compact-block-reconstructions/1052/63) - From mid-July to mid-October, 0xB10C's nodes could rebuild only 1% to 5% of new blocks from their own mempools and had to fetch about 100 kB per block from peers, which slows block propagation. Gregory Sanders found the missing transactions were nearly all rune mints: chains of 25 roughly 100-vbyte transactions paying about 10 sats each, built on pay-to-anchor (P2A) outputs and pushed by a fleet of peers with user agents like `/Sat0shi:31.0.0/`. A "sweeper" spent anchors partway down the chains, paying their whole value as fee and evicting the rest, so node mempools drifted away from what pools mined. It cleared up around October 18, when the fleet went quiet, timing that matches a fork of the alkanes protocol at block 972,000 ([BNOC thread](https://bnoc.xyz/t/compact-block-reconstruction-degradation-between-september-october-2026/198/)). In November a new wave of mints, too small to relay under the dust limit, started reaching ViaBTC, F2Pool and AntPool, apparently sent to them directly. If anyone can hand transactions straight to a pool, what can node relay policy still do?

#### Grants

- [OpenSats: 21 grants for Bitcoin software, hardware, and education](https://opensats.org/blog/21-grants-supporting-bitcoin-software-hardware-and-education) - Eight new grants and 13 renewals from the General Fund, announced November 17. On mining: bit-aloo on the Stratum V2 Reference Implementation (cleaning up the protocol libraries and adding deterministic simulation testing as Sv2 reaches the big pools) and a renewal for Braidpool, a peer-to-peer mining pool. On other node implementations: Libbitcoin v4 gets a built-in block explorer and admin dashboard, and Floresta's grants are renewed. Also: a Tor Project team hunting malicious relays, the Code Orange Dev School privacy developer track, Dana's silent payments wallet, Cashu-TS and Cashu.me, bdk-cli, and renewals for rust-bitcoin, Krux, Fully Noded, and others. With Libbitcoin, Floresta and rbitcoin all in active development, how many full node implementations can Bitcoin support?

### Lightning

#### Node Software

- [lnd v0.21.8-beta](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/releases/tag/v0.21.8-beta) - The November 11 release (with v0.20.8 for the older line) names no vulnerability, but its [release notes](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/blob/master/docs/release-notes/release-notes-0.21.8.md) are a long list of correctness fixes in places where funds are at stake: forwarded HTLCs recovered correctly across reconnects, settled AMP HTLCs recovered during on-chain resolution, peer channel aliases already assigned to another channel now rejected, a restart that could silently drop acknowledged channel updates, a peer deadlock, and force-close transactions that hit replacement-fee rejections. v0.21.7 is the same build under a corrected tag.
- [lnd v0.21.6-beta: CVE-2026-98432](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/blob/master/docs/release-notes/release-notes-0.21.6.md) - The named vulnerability is two releases back, and it is not in lnd. [CVE-2026-98432](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-98432) is a Linux kernel bug whose [demonstrated remote exploit](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/10/30/1) needs a Multipath TCP listener. Go 1.24+ turns MPTCP on by default, so lnd 0.21.x exposed one on its peer, gRPC and REST ports. The October 30 release stops opening MPTCP listeners. Linux operators should upgrade, disable MPTCP on the host (`sysctl -w net.mptcp.enabled=0`), and install a patched kernel, since the kernel bug is not specific to MPTCP. What else on your node's host listens with defaults you never chose?
- [Eclair, Core Lightning and LDK security releases](https://github.com/ACINQ/eclair/releases/tag/v0.14.6) - lnd was not the only implementation patching. Eclair shipped three security releases in October: 0.14.4 fixed splicing, HTLC settlement and on-the-fly funding bugs and now blocks API requests carrying a browser `Origin` header, [0.14.5](https://github.com/ACINQ/eclair/releases/tag/v0.14.5) closed a way for a malicious payer to steal from a trampoline-forwarding node and a way for a peer to get Eclair to sign an invalid taproot splice by lying about its inputs, and 0.14.6 hardens force-closes while splices are unconfirmed and stops splices from spending a peer's unconfirmed inputs by default. [Core Lightning 26.06.10](https://github.com/ElementsProject/lightning/releases/tag/v26.06.10), released November 5, fixes dual funding and splicing (including checks on the peer's `tx_signatures`), on-chain HTLC resolution, runes and commando, and again withholds the tests for its security fixes to slow down exploit writers. [LDK 0.2.8 and 0.1.14](https://github.com/lightningdevkit/rust-lightning/releases/tag/v0.2.8) fix fund-loss and denial-of-service bugs and cooperative-close problems. Splicing shows up in almost every list: is it where Lightning's attack surface has moved?

#### Self-Hosted Services

- [BTCPay hot wallets swept](https://decrypt.co/382324/btcpay-tells-merchants-to-switch-off-servers-amid-hot-wallet-attack-reports) - On November 1, BTCPay told operators to shut their servers down after reports that hot wallets were being swept by an attacker who logged in as an administrator. The servers hit appear to be running releases older than August's [2.4.2 fix](https://blog.btcpayserver.org/security-advisory-btcpay-server-2-4-2/) for an actively exploited bug, and as of November 2 BTCPay did not know how the attacker got admin access. Bitquery [traced](https://cryptoslate.com/btcpay-urges-bitcoin-merchants-to-replace-wallets-after-hackers-steal-an-estimated-1-6-million/) about $1.63 million, 18.38 BTC and 3,405 LTC, across 682 sweeps from October 30 to November 2, with another 267 sweeps after the warning went out. Operators are told to move remaining funds to a new wallet with a fresh seed. Three days before, BTCPay had shipped [2.4.7](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/releases/tag/v2.4.7), which fixes 85 findings from Anthropic's OSS Scanner, the service #55 discussed: 1 critical, 13 high, 52 medium and 19 low, reported with reproducers and proposed fixes. BTCPay has no credible reports of drains on 2.4.7, but has not said whether the attacker used any of those bugs.
- [LNbits: "shut down immediately"](https://news.lnbits.com/news/lnbits-1-7-0-security-update) - On Sunday, November 8, LNbits told operators to disable its Paywall extension, then about an hour later to shut down every server, after a wave of attacks drained funds. On one instance, newly created accounts withdrew about 2.3 million sats before the operator stopped it ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/lnbits-rushes-emergency-patch-after-reported-bitcoin-wallet-thefts)). LNbits took its own hosted instances offline too. [v1.7.0](https://github.com/lnbits/lnbits/releases/tag/v1.7.0), released November 10, fixes every earlier version, but upgrading is not enough: operators should reset all wallet keys, rotate funding-source credentials such as macaroons and API keys, treat every secret stored in LNbits as compromised, and recreate NWC, LNDHub and Bolt Card connections. The exploit itself has not been published yet. 1.7.0 also adds automatic extension updates. Both attacks hit servers running old code: should self-hosted payment software update itself?

### Ark

- [Noah: an Ark wallet rewritten native](https://github.com/smolcars/noah) - Noah, a trust-minimized Ark wallet built on Bark, was rewritten from React Native into native Swift (SwiftUI) and Kotlin (Jetpack Compose) with a full redesign. Claude Opus 5.5 did the rewrite in five days: [one pull request](https://github.com/smolcars/noah/pull/374) of about 333,000 added lines across 1,562 files ([announcement](https://x.com/nitesh_btc/status/2114547704424743292)). On a Pixel 6, cold start went from 4.3s to 1.4s, memory from 262 MB to 128 MB, and the APK from 105 MB to 61 MB, though scroll jank rose from 0.13% to 1.2% and is being fixed. On an iPhone 17, cold start went from 1.42s to 0.85s and memory from 105 MB to 30 MB. Still in beta. If an agent can rewrite an app natively in a week, is there still a case for cross-platform frameworks in Bitcoin wallets?
- [Bark 0.8.1: presigned emergency exits](https://x.com/secondhq/status/2120890608374120543) - Second's November 5 [release](https://gitlab.com/ark-bitcoin/bark/-/releases/bark-0.8.1) lets a wallet [sign its whole emergency exit ahead of time](https://gitlab.com/ark-bitcoin/bark/-/merge_requests/2293), including a CPFP child for every exit transaction, for as long as its confirmed on-chain funds last. The result is a wave-by-wave broadcast plan that cold storage or a third party can execute later, with the wallet offline. Barkd can also list every unconfirmed exit transaction in broadcast order, so a service can broadcast them and pay the fees for a user who holds no on-chain bitcoin. Also new: a `bark-cli` thin client that drives Barkd over REST. [Changelog](https://second.tech/docs/changelog).
- [A watchtower for Ark](https://x.com/notgrubles/status/2121743384461164676) - Grubles of Second put those presigned exits to work in a tower for wallets that stay offline until their VTXOs are about to expire. If the wallet's own refresh never happens, the tower runs its own Bitcoin node, watches the expiry, and broadcasts the presigned exit early enough to confirm. It holds no keys and can only move the coins on-chain to their owner. In a signet test, an offline wallet's coins [reached the chain](https://mempool.space/signet/tx/c968d4c45f2ff05784984f2618fcaab9cc8d44357f7bd9b503ece123a5bedc7e) 142 blocks before expiry. The design is early, and a public signet tower is planned. The fee rate is fixed when you sign: what happens if fees spike just as many VTXOs near expiry?
- [Wavelength is live on mainnet](https://lightning.engineering/posts/2026-10-23-wavelength-mainnet-launch/) - Lightning Labs' payments toolkit for apps and agents launched on mainnet on October 23, built on what it calls an "Ark-like settlement layer." It is self-custodial, needs no node or channels, and agents can drive the wallet over MCP, pay L402 APIs, and hold receive-only credentials. It is still an alpha, capped at 300,000 sats per account and 50,000 sats per VTXO, with a 0.25% service fee on Lightning sends and receives. Funds need a free renewal about every two weeks, and a wallet that misses it recovers its funds by working with the server when it comes back online. How does that compare with Bark's approach of exiting on-chain before expiry, and which trade-off would you want an agent's wallet to make?

### Markets and Policy

- [Binance's $6.3 billion hack](https://cointelegraph.com/news/recap-binances-record-63b-hack-unfolded) - On October 29, attackers drained Binance hot wallets across several chains, taking about $6.3 billion, the largest crypto theft on record. Binance says the attacker used an EIP-7702 delegation, an Ethereum feature that lets an account hand execution to code, to take control of a hot wallet without passing its normal approval controls. It says the breach went beyond the one compromised laptop first suspected, and that it found no evidence private keys leaked. TRM Labs attributes the attack to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, which the FBI blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025. Industry partners froze about $3 billion, Binance says it will cover the losses from its own funds and had replenished 90% of the stolen assets by October 31, and withdrawals reopened in stages on November 1, when DefiLlama counted more than $3.5 billion of net outflows. Blockaid's CEO said the attackers could have stolen the funds without EIP-7702, but that it may have helped them sweep everything at once. Bitcoin has nothing like account delegation: does that conservatism protect exchanges, or does a compromised signing system drain any chain?
- [Bitcoin's six-week slide](https://cryptobriefing.com/bitcoin-falls-23-november-2026-bear-market/) - Bitcoin bounced to $81,004 the day after the midterms, then fell to about $62,000 by mid-November: six straight weekly losses, and roughly half its all-time high of about $126,000 from October 2025. US spot bitcoin ETFs had about $1.3 billion of net outflows in the first week of November alone ([recap](https://cryptoticker.io/en/crypto-news-this-week-recap-key-dates-november-2026/)), more than $2 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated over one mid-November weekend, and in early November futures priced a likely Fed rate hike on December 9. Miners feel it first: hashprice fell to about $32 per PH/s per day by November 9 ([Hashrate Index](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/hashrate-index-roundup-november-9-2026/)). Comparisons with November 2022 are everywhere. Does a bear market change what gets built, or who is left building it?
- [Democrats sweep the midterms](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/clarity-act-faces-new-test-under-democratic-congress) - Democrats won both the House and the Senate on November 3 and take control in January, with Elizabeth Warren expected to chair Senate Banking. The CLARITY Act, the market structure bill the House passed in July 2025, had already stalled when the Senate failed to advance it 49 to 50 in September, and it dies with this Congress on January 3 unless the lame-duck Senate passes it ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382515/bitcoin-rebounds-democratic-midterm-sweep-crypto-washington)). Critics are already citing the Binance hack as a case for tougher exchange custody rules. The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve bill, ARMA (H.R. 8957), cleared House Financial Services 28 to 21 on a party-line vote in September but has not passed the full House, and Patrick Witt, former executive director of the President's Council of Advisors for Digital Assets, [said on November 9](https://cryptobriefing.com/strategic-bitcoin-reserve-needs-congress-approval/) that the reserve cannot buy bitcoin on the open market without Congress.
